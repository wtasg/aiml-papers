## Vocab

| Word | Meaning |
| - | - |
| Neuron | The fundamental cell of the nervous system; a specialized cell that receives, processes, and transmits information through electrical and chemical signals. |
| Axon | A usually long and single nerve-cell process that conducts impulses away from the cell body to other neurons, muscles, or glands. |
| Soma | The cell body of a neuron or glial cell that contains the cell nucleus; the central part of a neuron that integrates incoming signals. |
| Synapse | The junction between two cells where nerve impulses pass; the site of communication between a presynaptic neuron and a postsynaptic cell, neuron, or effector. |
| Excitation | A postsynaptic response caused by neurotransmitters binding to receptors that increases the probability that an action potential will be produced. |
| Inhibition | The opposite of excitation; a postsynaptic response that decreases the probability that an action potential will occur; interruption or blockade of neuronal activity. |
| Internuncial | A nerve cell that carries signals from one nerve cell to another; a type of interneuron found primarily in the central nervous system. |
| Afferent | Bearing or conducting inward; specifically conveying impulses toward the central nervous system; sensory nerves. |
| Efferent | Conducting outward from a part or organ; specifically conveying nervous impulses to an effector; motor nerves. |

+ Neuron: Often classified as sensory (afferent), motor (efferent), or interneuron (internuncial).
+ Excitation vs. Inhibition: The balance between these two forces is crucial for proper neural circuit function and stability.
+ Afferent vs. Efferent:
  + Afferent = incoming/sensory → toward CNS
  + Efferent = outgoing/motor → away from CNS
+ Internuncial neurons are also called association neurons or connector neurons—they link different parts of the CNS together.
